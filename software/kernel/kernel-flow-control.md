# Kernel flow-control routines, one entry each

Release IRIX 6.5.30m, the flow-control credit manager `gfx_flow.o` inside the support archive `gfxs.a` of the IP30 and IP35 kernel builds. The VPro driver calls these routines to give a board a share of the host's write-response credits and to bind a graphics process to that share (`buzz/README.md` section 1 compares the two hosts). Each entry starts with the routine, its member, offset in `.text` and size in bytes, then names the build; an entry speaks for that build only. Labels are **established** (the whole routine was read from the disassembly) or **inferred**.

On IP30 every routine addresses the host chip through the uncached addresses 0xaff00000 and 0xaff10000, physical 0x0ff00000 and 0x0ff10000. The Linux header `arch/mips/include/asm/sgi/heart.h` (`struct ip30_heart_regs`, mainline Linux) puts these registers at the HEART base: `fc_mode` at +0x40, `fc_timer_limit` at +0x48, two `fc_addr` registers at +0x50, two `fc_credit_cnt` registers at +0x60, `status` at +0x80, `clear_isr` at +0x10028, and it names interrupt bits 1 and 2 `HEART_L0_INT_FLOW_CTRL_HWTR_0` and `_1`. Linux marks the purpose of the flow-control registers as unknown ("possibly for GFX flow control"), so the reading of each field below is **inferred**, while the addresses and values are read from the code. The routines `heart_flow_control_connect`, `heart_flow_control_disconnect`, `heart_flow_control_enable` and `heart_flow_control_disable` are imports: the shipped `kernel.o` does not define them, so their bodies are **unknown** here and no entry reads them.

Several routines read the halfword at the fixed kernel address 0xffffffffffffc3a0, loaded as `lh` from -15456 off the zero register. It holds the number of the processor running the routine, which is **inferred** from its use as the index of the per-processor owner slot and of the two per-processor HEART register pairs. The entries call it the processor number.

## IP30 build (`usr/cpu/sysgen/IP30boot/gfxs.a(gfx_flow.o)`)

The credit record is the 104-byte block that `gfx_flow_alloc` obtains with `kmem_alloc`; its words are named by offset below. `gfx_flow_owners` is a three-doubleword table that `gfx_flow_init` allocates, one slot per processor number; `gfx_flow_heart_lock` is a spin lock for the read-modify-write of `fc_mode`; `gfx_flow_init_lock` serialises the first allocation and the last release.

- `gfx_flow_delayed_hiwater_intr_prehandler` (`gfx_flow.o`, offset 0x0, 136 bytes): **established.**
  Build: `usr/cpu/sysgen/IP30boot/gfxs.a(gfx_flow.o)`.
  First-level handler for the flow-control high-water interrupt that `gfx_flow_alloc` connects for channels 0 and 1. Read in full, both the unexpected-owner path and the dispatch path. Its first argument (a0) indexes `gfx_flow_owners` by eight bytes. When the owner slot is zero it logs two messages at severity 4 through `cmn_err` ("gfx_flow_delayed_hiwater_intr_prehandler: unexpected intr on cpu %d", then "disabling flow control"), takes `gfx_flow_heart_lock`, and clears the two bits 0x300 (bits 8 and 9) of the doubleword at 0xaff00040, the store landing in the delay slot of `mutex_spinunlock`; it then returns. When the slot holds a record, it calls the function pointer at record +40 with the doubleword at record +48 as the first argument and 0 as the second, and returns.

## IP35 build (`usr/cpu/sysgen/IP35boot/gfxs.a(gfx_flow.o)`)

This build touches the host through the 64-bit constants of its `.lit8` section, which the object carries as plain data: `.lit8` +0x00 0x9200000001000000 (called the base below), +0x08 0x9200000001820000, +0x10 0x9200000001a20000, +0x18 0x9200000001800000, +0x20 0x4000000400000007, +0x28 0x9200000001c00140, +0x30 0x6dffffffff000000, +0x38 0x00000000deadbabe, +0x40 0x9200000001000090, +0x48 0x9200000001000098 and +0x50 0x9200000001030100. Several routines read the halfword at the fixed kernel address 0xffffffffffffc428 (the processor number n) and use `pdaindr`: the pointer at 16 times n plus 8 in that table leads to a per-processor record whose byte at +1320 has bit 0, here s, that selects one of two register groups 40 bytes apart. The group's registers are at the base plus 768, 776, 784 and 792, plus 40 times s. No public source names this block; its role is **inferred** from the accesses below: +776 receives the credit total at attach and is read back as the credit count, +784 is cleared before and after, +768 receives a configuration doubleword, and +792 is cleared by the interrupt entry.

The credit record is 112 bytes from `gfx_flow_alloc`. `gfx_flow_igfx_owners` holds two doubleword slots per node (16 bytes), `gfx_flow_cpu_attachments` one doubleword per processor, and `gfx_flow_attach_count` counts live attachments.
