# Odyssey driver routines of `libGLcore.so`, read one by one

Release IRIX 6.5.30m, N32 `libGLcore.so` for IP30 (`usr/gfx/arch/IP30ODSY32/libGLcore.so`; the IP35 copy and the patch builds carry the same routines where their code hashes agree in `docs/inventory/functions.tsv`). Each entry names a routine of the board-specific `ODYSSEY` directory (`libglcore/asm/ODYSSEY/`), its address and size, and says what it computes. Labels are **established** for routines whose whole body was read in the Ghidra decompilation (`$gp` and jump tables resolved with `ghidra/scripts/IrixGp.java` and `IrixJumpTables.java`) next to the assembly, and **inferred** for statements that rest on a callee that was not read. Offsets are into the Odyssey GL context record (the record the library keeps its current context in, address 0x1010 of the process), unless an entry says another record. The routines that move pixels, draw and copy pixel rectangles, transfer depth and stencil, and set the raster position belong to other chapters and are not read here. The same routines in the other builds are listed in [libglcore-odyssey-builds.md](libglcore-odyssey-builds.md).

Words sent to the board are the class-1 command words of `BUZZ.h`: `0x00010000 | COUNT << 10 | REG`, followed by COUNT operands. "The settle sequence" is the group `0x10443` with operand 250, `0x10046` twice and `0x10019`, `0x10443` with operand 150 and `0x10046` four times, and `0x10443` with operand 250 and `0x10046` twice, as in `docs/opengl.md` (the X module's draw routines send the same words). Writes go to the command-FIFO window the library maps at process address 0x10000.

Dispatch-table slots. The context holds a pointer at +0x17f4 to a table of 4-byte entry points in the order of the GL entry names (`__glim_Vertex2f` calls slot +0x534, the `Vertex2fv` entry, with a pointer to two floats). Names given for slots below are **inferred** from that order together with the width and count of the arguments each caller passes.

## Validation and defaults

- `__glOdysseyValidateHardware` (`builds/6.5.30-media/usr/gfx/arch/IP30ODSY32/libGLcore.so`, 0xd968180, 4992 bytes): **established.**
  Pushes every piece of changed context state to the board as class-1 blocks, choosing the blocks by the dirty word at +0x16960 (read at entry; set to 0 at exit). Before each block it tests the "settle needed" word at +0x9830 and, when set, clears it and sends the settle prefix `0x10443` 250, `0x10046`, `0x10046`. The bit-to-block map, each block's operands taken from the context at the offset shown: bit 1 sends `0x10404` with the word at +0x7bfc and bit 2 sends `0x10405` with the word at +0x7c00; bit 0 sends `0x10c21` with three words from +0x7c34; bit 3 sends `0x1181a` with six words from +0x7c08; bit 4 sends `0x1081d` with the two words at +0x7c20; bit 5 sends `0x1041f` with the word at +0x7c2c; bit 6 sends `0x1084a` with the two words at +0x7c54; bit 7 sends `0x10452` with the word at +0x7c5c; bit 8 sends `0x1141b` with five words from +0x7cb0; bit 9 sends `0x11c16` with seven words from +0x7cc4; bit 13 sends `0x11847` with six words from +0x970c; bit 14 sends `0x11013` with four words from +0x9724; bit 15 sends `0x10c3e` with three words from +0x8d6c.
  The wider groups: bit 10 sends 64 level descriptors, blocks 0x280 to 0x2bf of three words each from +0x7ce0 (twelve-byte stride); bit 11 sends `0x10406` with the word at +0x7fe0; bit 12 sends the settle sequence, `0x10018` with no operand, and the settle sequence again; bits 18, 19 and 20 each send sixteen blocks of sixteen words, blocks 0x220 to 0x22f from +0x7fe4, 0x230 to 0x23f from +0x83e4 and 0x240 to 0x24f from +0x87e4; bit 22 sends two runs of sixteen blocks of sixteen words, blocks 0x200 to 0x20f from +0x8d7c and 0x210 to 0x21f from +0x917c; bit 23 sends `0x10c1c` with three words from +0x957c; bit 24 sends `0x10451` with the word at +0x9698; bit 25 sends `0x10415` with the word at +0x7c04; bit 21 sends the settle sequence and then either `0x90008116` or, when bit 27 of the word at +0x96a4 is set, blocks 0x041, 0x04b and 0x040 with zero operands (the second with 0x00ffffff in its second operand), followed by `0x11017` with four words from +0x969c.
  Bits 16 and 17 choose among three layouts by a mode word. Bit 16 reads the mode at +0x8ce8: mode 1 sends blocks 0x030 to 0x033 of eight words each from +0x8cec (0x20-byte stride), mode 2 sends block 0x03b with eight words from +0x8cec, mode 3 sends `0x1083d` with two words.   The contents of blocks 0x028 to 0x02b, 0x038 and 0x03a are **unknown**; `docs/buzz.md` (the second three-layout set) lists the routes tried.

- `__glOdysseyValidateDispatchTable` (`builds/6.5.30-media/usr/gfx/arch/IP30ODSY32/libGLcore.so`, 0xd970d30, 1844 bytes): **established.**

For modes 2 and 4 it points the context's primitive-begin entries (+0x2f34 to +0x2f58) at the soft-clip forms (`__glOdysseySoftClipBeginPoints` and its siblings) and the procedure pickers (+0x2e48 to +0x2e94) at the soft-clip pickers, using the fast triangle picker when the bytes at +0x19de2 and +0xc94 allow. For the other modes it writes the generic `__glBegin*` entries and pickers. It ends by setting the global at address 0x1018 to 2 and bit 0 of the word at +0x2e24, which flag that validation has more to do.
- `__glOdysseyValidateMatrixComb` (`builds/6.5.30-media/usr/gfx/arch/IP30ODSY32/libGLcore.so`, 0xd9e2f90, 1652 bytes): **established.**
  Rebuilds the combined viewport and projection numbers the board needs after a transform or viewport change. It forms two four-float vectors at +0x9588 to +0x95a4 from the matrix record at +0x7414 and the viewport words at +0x6b8 to +0x6c4, calls `__glOdysseyUpdateTransform` first when the byte at +0x16740 asks for it, and then computes, from the near and far distances at +0x165c8 and +0x165d0, the cached scale terms at +0x165f8 to +0x16610 and the words at +0x6c8 and +0x6cc, the rows stored at +0x95a8 to +0x95d4 (each a combination of the matrix's third and fourth columns, +0xb8 to +0xec of the record); the matrix's fourth-column terms +0xbc, +0xcc, +0xdc and +0xec are copied unchanged to +0x95b8 to +0x95c4. The 20 words from +0x9588 to +0x95d4 go to the board as follows: `0x34008000` selecting table index 0 of register 0x34, the word `0x9080bdc8` and the sixteen floats from +0x9588, then `0x34008000 | enc(16)`, the word `0x908091df` and the four floats from +0x95c8, then a reselect of index 0 (these are the register-0x34 table slots that `docs/buzz.md` lists for the kernel's restore).
  It then sets the projection class at +0x19dbc (the same classes `__glOdysseyValidateDispatchTable` reads): when the byte at +0x19dc0 is set it takes 0 if the byte at +0x19db4 is clear, 3 if the word at +0x19db8 is 0x1102 and 2 otherwise. When +0x19db4 is set and +0x19db8 is 0x1101 it picks 2 if any of +0xbc, +0xcc and +0xdc is nonzero, and when all three are zero it picks 2 or 1 by comparing the magnitude of +0xec with the float at +0x19d90, setting the word at +0x16738 to 1 in the all-zero cases. - `__glOdysseyUpdateTransform` (`builds/6.5.30-media/usr/gfx/arch/IP30ODSY32/libGLcore.so`, 0xd9e2980, 1544 bytes): **established.**
  Analyses the current projection matrix and stores the eye-space distances the depth mapping needs. It reads the projection matrix record at +0x741c and the model-view record at +0x7414. When the byte at +0x19dcc is set, the projection's flag word at +0x40 is 4 and the model-view record has a nonzero term at +0xc, +0x1c or +0x2c, it copies the 4 x 4 at +0xb0 of that record with its fourth column set to 0, 0, 0 and 1.0, takes its inverse transpose with `__glInvertTransposeMatrix`, multiplies the projection's fourth row (+0xc, +0x1c, +0x2c, +0x3c) through it to get the four terms used below, and sets the byte at +0x19dcd to 1. Otherwise it clears that byte and reads the matrix terms directly, replacing a projection with a skew by its scale factors, the square roots of the sums of squares of the column terms at +0x8, +0x18 and +0x28 and at +0xc, +0x1c and +0x2c, signed by the sign of the diagonal entries +0x28 and +0x2c.
  The four numbers it ends with (the two scales and the two translations) are cached in doubles at +0x165f8, +0x16600, +0x16608 and +0x16610 and the routine reuses them without recomputing when the terms at +0x28, +0x2c, +0x38 and +0x3c already equal the cached values and the terms at +0x8, +0xc, +0x18 and +0x1c are zero. From them it derives the near and far distances n and f and writes them at +0x165c8 and +0x165d0, picking between the formulas for an orthographic matrix (the second scale zero) and a perspective matrix (the sum and difference of the scales). It sets the word at +0x1673c to 1 when the matrix at +0x7428 has a nonzero perspective column, clears the byte at +0x16740, calls the unnamed routine at 0xd9e4d90, and calls `__glOdyim_PolygonOffsetEXT` again when either of the offset factors at +0x1f4 and +0x1f8 is nonzero, so that the polygon offset follows the new depth scale.

- `__glOdysseyValidateFog` (`builds/6.5.30-media/usr/gfx/arch/IP30ODSY32/libGLcore.so`, 0xd980cd0, 1800 bytes): **established.**
  Turns the GL fog state into the three fog words of block 0x1a (+0x7c10, +0x7c14, +0x7c18 and +0x7c1c) and marks dirty bit 8. Fog is described to the board as a cubic curve through four control values over the eye-distance range. It calls `__glOdysseyUpdateTransform` first when the byte at +0x16740 asks, and takes n and f from +0x165c8 and +0x165d0. By the mode word at +0x634: for GL_EXP (0x800) and GL_EXP2 (0x801) it takes the density at +0x648, limits the range end to 3.912 / density for exp (where the factor is 0.02) and to 1.83096 / density for exp2, samples exp at the range's start, one third, two thirds and end, and turns the four samples into cubic control values with the interior ones (18 s1 - 9 s2 + 2 s3 - 5 s0) / 6 and (18 s2 - 9 s1 - 5 s3 + 2 s0) / 6; for GL_LINEAR (0x2601) the control values are the straight line 1, 2/3, 1/3, 0 or its reverse, with the start and end at +0x64c and +0x650 and the slope word at +0x654 choosing the direction; for 0x812a (the fog-function extension) the four values come from +0x16454 to +0x16460, the scale from +0x16468 and the offset from +0x16464. When bit 4 of +0x704 is set it subtracts the offset word at +0x16478.
  It then converts to window depth using the depth range doubles at +0x6a8 and +0x6b0, folds the scale and offset into an exponent (a 3-bit power of two chosen with `logf` and `ceilf` so the offset stays within +-8) and packs: bits 23:0 of +0x7c10 take the float bits of the scale rebiased by 0xd0000000 and shifted right 5, bits 11:0 of +0x7c14 the offset times 256 and bits 14:12 the exponent, +0x7c18 and +0x7c1c take the control values times 256 in 12-bit fields (first and second control value in +0x7c18 bits 23:12 and 11:0, third and fourth in +0x7c1c bits 23:12 and 11:0). An offset below -8 or above 7.996 is brought into range by the exponent, and one beyond -1024 or 1023.5 is replaced by -8 or 7.996 with the scale reduced by the same ratio.

## Primitive replay for the clip path

- `__glOdysseyVertexWARClip` (`builds/6.5.30-media/usr/gfx/arch/IP30ODSY32/libGLcore.so`, 0xd9ef330, 4452 bytes): **established.**
  Called with one vertex (four float arguments) when a primitive started on the board must be finished through the software clip path; it ends the board's primitive, rebuilds the transform state the software path needs, and re-issues the primitive's held vertices through the immediate-mode entry points, ending with the vertex it was called for. It first asks the hardware-shadow routine for the board's primitive state (`__glOdysseyGetHardwareShadow`) and copies 0x900 bytes of it to its stack. The word at +0x80 of that copy holds the primitive mode in bits 3:0 (a value of 10 or more makes it print a message and send nothing), the number of vertices held in bits 5:4, the index of the oldest held vertex in bits 7:6 (the held vertices form a ring of four) and a strip-parity flag in bit 11.
  It sets the byte at +0x169fe, for mode 2 sets the settle flag at +0x9830 and sends `0x10444` with operand 0, calls the dispatch table's end entry (+0x2c, inferred End) and zeroes the pair at +0x80 and +0x84 of the record at +0x6d4. The words at +0x88 and +0x8c of that record take -1.0 and 1.0 when the byte at +0x19dcd is set, and otherwise a unit step whose sign follows the order of the far and near distances at +0x165d0 and +0x165c8, and that step multiplied by n. When the projection class at +0x19dbc is 3 it also writes four clip planes into the record at +0x6d4, from +0x90 to +0xcc: for the x and y rows of the projection matrix at +0x741c the planes are 0.9 times the row's x (or y) term plus its w term, and w minus 0.9 times the term, so they sit at 1/0.9 of the viewing volume's x and y bounds (the constant is 0.9 in the library's double pool at 0xdaf4fc0). ## Pipe-memory object bookkeeping

Every context keeps the host's picture of the board memory objects it uses. Three structures, all in the context record: a ring of contexts that share one kernel attach record (next-pointer at +0x16c70), a "known group" set at +0x16c78 whose elements are the group records below, and a pointer at +0x16c94 to the attach record the kernel shares between those contexts, which holds a second set of the sharing contexts themselves. The sets are chunked sparse arrays ("c-sets"): a circular doubly linked list of nodes, each node a 16-byte header (table pointer at +0, number of used slots at +4, next at +8, previous at +0xc) over a table of 2^shift words, with the set header holding the list head at +0, a cursor node at +4, the node count at +8, the element count at +0xc, a cursor slot index at +0x10, a cursor-finished flag at +0x14 and the shift at +0x18. A group record is 0x54 bytes: its id at +0, the kernel's object handle halfword at +4, the object address at +8, sixteen list heads at +0xc to +0x4c (one list of range records per level count 0 to 15) and a word at +0x50. A range record is 0x2c bytes: next at +0, its group at +4, level at +8, covered count at +0xc, a read-busy flag at +0x10, a write-busy flag at +0x14, two words preset to -1 at +0x18 and +0x1c, a status-slot index at +0x20, a counter index at +0x22, a pointer to the 16-byte counter record at +0x24 and a pointer to a 16-bit status slot at +0x28. Object numbers -3 and -4 never have group records; they stand for the kernel's attach record (see `__glOdysseyOpenPMObjReference` above). Every routine below calls the context's allocator and release entries (allocate at +0, allocate-count-by-size at +4, release at +0xc) through the process-global context pointer or the context argument.

## Bounding-box test for display lists

- `__glOdysseyIsBboxClippedRange` (`builds/6.5.30-media/usr/gfx/arch/IP30ODSY32/libGLcore.so`, 0xd973750, 3496 bytes): **established** for the arithmetic, **inferred** for the names of the box fields and of the matrix terms.
  Returns 1 when a display list's bounding box cannot be skipped as inside the view, 0 when the box's whole projection is small enough (arguments: context, a nine-float box record). When the word at +0x16738 is set it hands over to `__glOdysseyIsBboxClippedRange2` and returns its answer. Otherwise there are three linear forms of a corner (x, y, z): w from the row at +0x95b8, +0x95bc, +0x95c0 (constant +0x95c4); a depth term from the matrix record at +0x7414 (x, y, z coefficients at +0xb8, +0xc8, +0xd8 and constant +0xe8, the third column of the 4 x 4 at +0xb0, the column `__glOdysseyValidateMatrixComb` above combines with the fourth); and two screen terms from the rows at +0x9588 (constant +0x9594) and +0x9598 (constant +0x95a4). The box floats 0 to 5 are x min, x max, y min, y max, z min and z max, in that order (the edge lengths are formed as max minus min). Phase one starts at the corner (x min, y min, z min), evaluates w and the depth term there, and visits the other seven corners along a Gray-code path, one edge per step (+x, +y, +z, -y, -x, +y, -z; corner numbers 0, 4, 6, 7, 5, 1, 3, 2 with bit 2 = x max, bit 1 = y max, bit 0 = z max), tracking the corner whose depth term over w is smallest by cross-multiplied comparisons (no division); with w negative the comparison is made on the negated values. If w has opposite signs at two of the corners it returns 1 straight away (the box straddles the eye plane). Phase two starts at the chosen corner and walks all eight corners again along the same path, with each edge step negated when the start corner is already at that axis's maximum. Each edge length is first limited: when the largest of the three floats at +0x18, +0x1c and +0x20 of the record (box floats 6 to 8) is not above the z edge length, all three edge lengths become that largest value (which of the box's fields these are was not traced; **inferred** to be a half-size or radius used as an upper bound). At each corner it forms the two screen terms and w and keeps, for each screen axis, the corners with the smallest and the largest screen term over w (cross-multiplied again). The result is 1 when either axis's extent (largest minus smallest screen term over w, now divided) exceeds the float at +0x19d94 times the constant 2.0 (0xdaf4b40), and 0 otherwise. In the normalised units of x over w that threshold is a multiple of the 2.0 width of the view, so the test says the box projects larger than that multiple of the viewport.

## Enable, disable and enable queries

The three routines below are the board-specific halves of `glEnable`, `glDisable` and `glIsEnabled`: the generic library handles the enumerants the board has no state for, and these keep the host's picture of the enable bits that the board's state blocks are built from. All three read the context from the process address 0x1010 and refuse to run inside a glBegin and glEnd pair (the global at 0x1018 equals 1): they raise GL_INVALID_OPERATION (0x502) and return. An enumerant they do not know raises GL_INVALID_ENUM (0x500). The compiler turned each switch into a binary search, so no jump table is involved (`ghidra/scripts/IrixJumpTables.java` found none in either); the enumerant-to-bit tables below were read from the decompilation and then checked by running both routines in an emulator (Unicorn, the library's own code with a zeroed context, once with all state bits clear and once with all set so that cleared bits show; neighbours of each enumerant were probed to catch range tests). Enumerant names are from the public OpenGL registry. Unless a row says otherwise, a successful case also stores 2 in the global at 0x1018 ("state changed since the last validation"), which the next draw call turns into a validation. Words: +0x700 and +0x704 are the two main enable words, +0x708 the light enables, +0x70c the user clip-plane enables, +0x710 and +0x714 the map enables, +0x718 the asynchronous-operation flags, +0x7bfc the board's raster-control word (the first block `__glOdysseyValidateHardware` sends, `0x10404`), +0x16960 and +0x16964 the two dirty words.

## Attribute stack

glPushAttrib and glPopAttrib are kept in two parallel pointer stacks: the hardware-state records (stack array at +0x168d0, top pointer at +0x168d4, depth limit at +0xec0 entries) and the generic-state records (base at +0x31b8, top at +0x31bc). A push fills a pair of records, a 0xed50-byte one for the board's state and a 0xbd0-byte one for the generic GL state, both allocated with the context's calloc entry (+4) the first time a stack slot is used; the first word of the generic record keeps the attribute mask. In what follows "rec1" is the 0xed50-byte record and "rec2" the 0xbd0-byte one. The mask bits are the public GL attribute bits: 0x1 current, 0x2 point, 0x4 line, 0x8 polygon, 0x10 polygon stipple, 0x20 pixel mode, 0x40 lighting, 0x80 fog, 0x100 depth buffer, 0x200 accumulation buffer, 0x400 stencil buffer, 0x800 viewport, 0x1000 transform, 0x2000 enable, 0x4000 colour buffer, 0x8000 hint, 0x10000 evaluator, 0x20000 list, 0x40000 texture, 0x80000 scissor.

## Accumulation buffer

The context keeps one accumulation implementation, chosen when the buffer is set up: a 16-bit accumulation buffer is a host float RGBA array that the library adds into itself, and a 24-bit one is kept on the board in two scratch images and combined with the blender. The context's entry for the operation (+0x11e24) holds the routine, and `__glOdyim_Accum` calls it. The soft buffer record sits at +0x7ab8 (data pointer at +0x10, bytes per pixel at +0x18, row stride in pixels at +0x1c, allocator at +0x84); the accumulation rectangle is the four floats at +0x11e28 (x min), +0x11e2c (y min), +0x11e30 (x max) and +0x11e34 (y max). The accumulation operations use the public enumerants GL_ACCUM (0x100), GL_LOAD (0x101), GL_RETURN (0x102), GL_MULT (0x103) and GL_ADD (0x104). These routines move pixels through the pixel paths (`docs/pixel-paths.md`) and are read here only as far as their own arithmetic and state go: `softAccumulate`, `softLoad`, `softReturn`, `hardLoadAccumulate24`, `hardReturn24` and `hardClear24` are the pixel-path callers and are not read in this pass.

## Per-state setters and the board words they build

These routines keep the GL value in the generic part of the context and build the board's own encoding in the hardware-state words. ## Draw-buffer, material and polygon-stipple setters

The routines below were read from the decompilation next to the assembly and then run in an emulator one input at a time (`scripts/glcore_sweep.py` for the decision trees, `scripts/fifo_words.py` for the command words). The emulator keeps 32-bit floating-point registers, so the shininess and colour-index branches of the two material-parameter routines, which hold doubles in odd-numbered registers, were read from the assembly and their command words checked by hand.

- `__glOdyim_DrawBuffer` (`builds/6.5.30-media/usr/gfx/arch/IP30ODSY32/libGLcore.so`, 0xd95bad0, 1356 bytes): **established.**
  glDrawBuffer (argument: the buffer enumerant). Inside glBegin and glEnd (global 0x1018 equal to 1) it raises GL_INVALID_OPERATION (0x502). The enumerants are 0, GL_FRONT_LEFT to GL_BACK_RIGHT (0x400 to 0x403), GL_FRONT, GL_BACK, GL_LEFT, GL_RIGHT, GL_FRONT_AND_BACK (0x404 to 0x408) and GL_AUX0 to GL_AUX3 (0x409 to 0x40c); anything else raises GL_INVALID_ENUM (0x500). Which ones are allowed depends on three bytes and a count of the visual: the stereo byte +0xc97, the back-buffer byte +0xc96, the byte +0x7b74 that decides whether the left and right selectors also take the back buffers, and the auxiliary-buffer count +0xce0. A forbidden one raises 0x502. On success it stores the effective buffer in +0x760, calls `__glOdysseySetDrawbuf` with four flags (front left, front right, back left, back right) and then stores the requested enumerant in +0x764. With the stereo byte set every enumerant keeps its own name in +0x760 and the flags follow the name: 0x400 to 0x403 one flag each, 0x404 front left and right, 0x405 back left and right, 0x406 and 0x407 front left or right (plus back left or right when +0x7b74 is set), 0x408 all four when +0x7b74 is set and front left and right otherwise. ## Fast-dispatch code buffers and their installation

The library's generated entry points for the current-value calls (colour, normal, texture coordinate, vertex) sit in one code region that `InitFastDispatch` makes writable. The routines below fill that region with one of several prepared sets of code, depending on the projection class, so a call such as `glColor3f` runs the code written for the current visual. The process record at the fixed address 0x17b4 holds the bookkeeping: +4 is the start of the region (the address of `glColor3b`), +0 its length, +8 the installed set (-1 for none), +0xc, +0x18, +0x20 and +0x28 the sizes 0x1400, 0x500, 0x1000 and 0x1800 of the colour, normal, texture-coordinate and vertex sets, and +0x10, +0x14, +0x1c, +0x24, +0x2c, +0x30 and +0x34 the pointers to the prepared copies (colour, colour index, normal, texture coordinate, vertex set 0, vertex set 2, vertex set 1).

- `CopyColorIndexCode` (`builds/6.5.30-media/usr/gfx/arch/IP30ODSY32/libGLcore.so`, 0xd966080, 1032 bytes): **established.**
  Lays 40 code templates end to end in one buffer (arguments: the destination, the table of template addresses passed by value in the argument registers and on the stack, and the size of one template on the stack). It calls `memcpy` 39 times and ends with a tail call of the 40th, each copy the template size (0x80 bytes in the caller `AllocInitFastDispatchCodeBuffers`, which gives a 0x1400-byte set) and each starting where the last ended. Which table it is handed decides whether the result serves the RGBA colour calls or the colour-index calls.
- `CopyNormalCode` (`builds/6.5.30-media/usr/gfx/arch/IP30ODSY32/libGLcore.so`, 0xd966490, 312 bytes): **established.**
  The same copy for the normal calls: ten templates (nine `memcpy` calls and a tail call of the tenth) from the table in `__glOdyim_normalDispatchTable`, each the size in its last argument (0x80 bytes in the caller), 0x500 bytes in all.
- `CopyTexCoordCode` (`builds/6.5.30-media/usr/gfx/arch/IP30ODSY32/libGLcore.so`, 0xd9665d0, 840 bytes): **established.**
  The same copy for the texture-coordinate calls: 32 templates (31 `memcpy` calls and a tail call of the last) from `__glOdyim_texCoordDispatchTable`, 0x80 bytes each in the caller, which fills the 0x1000-byte set.
- `CopyVertexCode` (`builds/6.5.30-media/usr/gfx/arch/IP30ODSY32/libGLcore.so`, 0xd966920, 680 bytes): **established.**
  Copies the vertex templates in two parts chosen by a flag word on the stack (arguments: the destination, the table by value, the template size 0x100 and the flags). Bit 0 copies the first sixteen templates, bit 1 copies the last eight after them, so a flag of 3 fills a 0x1800-byte set and a flag of 1 or 2 only its first 0x1000 or last 0x800 bytes. It makes 24 `memcpy` calls in all and returns after the last.
- `AllocInitFastDispatchCodeBuffers` (`builds/6.5.30-media/usr/gfx/arch/IP30ODSY32/libGLcore.so`, 0xd966bd0, 2124 bytes): **established.**
  Builds, on first use, the prepared code sets for one fast-dispatch mode (arguments: a record whose first word is the host's allocator callback, and a pointer to the mode number, which it rewrites). A negative mode does nothing. Otherwise any of the colour (+0x10), colour-index (+0x14), normal (+0x1c) and texture-coordinate (+0x24) sets that the process record at 0x17b4 does not hold yet is allocated through the callback with the size in the record and filled with `CopyColorIndexCode` (from `__glOdyim_colorDispatchTable` and `__glOdyim_indexDispatchTable`), `CopyNormalCode` and `CopyTexCoordCode`, and the vertex set for the mode is built into the record's slot: mode 0 at +0x2c from `__glOdyim_vertexDispatchTable` with flag 3, mode 2 at +0x30 from `__glOdyim_vertexSmallWDispatchTable` with flag 3, and mode 1 at +0x34 as the first part of the first table (flag 1) followed 0x1000 bytes on by the second part of the small-w table (flag 2). A failed allocation stores -1 in the mode and returns.
- `FastDispatchInstall` (`builds/6.5.30-media/usr/gfx/arch/IP30ODSY32/libGLcore.so`, 0xd967620, 724 bytes): **established.**
  Switches the code region to the set the context now needs (argument: the context). It does nothing unless the byte at +0x19de0 is set. The wanted mode is -1 when the word at +0x137c is nonzero, or when the word at +0x16988 has a bit other than bit 4, the process word at 0x1048 is nonzero, the current-context word at 0x1010 is zero or the projection class at +0x19dbc is 3; otherwise it is the class. When the wanted mode is the installed one (record +8) it returns. A non-negative mode first calls `AllocInitFastDispatchCodeBuffers`, whose result may fall back to -1. With -1 and a set installed it copies the saved original code back over the region (from the copy `InitFastDispatch` keeps at gp-13604) and clears the word at 0x17b8. With a mode, when nothing is installed yet (record +8 is -1) it first copies the colour set (+0x10 when the RGBA byte +0xc94 is set, +0x14 otherwise), the normal set and the texture-coordinate set one after another from the region's start; for any mode it then copies the mode's vertex set (0x1800 bytes, the slot of mode 0, 1 or 2) to the address of `glVertex2d` and sets 0x17b8 to 1, and on that first install it adds 0x80 bytes each from the entries at +0xa0 and +0xa4 of the colour or index table behind the vertex code. It ends with `cacheflush` over the region (protection argument 3) and stores the new mode at record +8.
